# Q0.2 Reference graph — contract

Status: infrastructure scaffold, **no canonical content imported**.

`corpus/registry.py` validates a JSON registry with `{"records":[...]}`. Every record requires a unique `id`, `kind`, `edition`, HTTPS `source_url`, content SHA-256, `verification` and `rights`. Quran references declare **riwayah** and **verse_numbering**; mushaf pages reference a Quran edition, page and riwayah; tafsir records identify work, author, and cited ayat. Edition-specific layouts must not be assumed to match across riwayat.

**Quality**: `staged → structurally_checked → source_verified → scholarly_verified`. These states record actual external checks, not inferred trust. Registry referential integrity alone is not authentication. To publish a text corpus, the verified edition must also independently carry `redistribution_permitted`.

**Rights**: `unknown / restricted / internal_only / redistribution_permitted`. Metadata and remote citations may remain usable when content rights are unresolved, subject to the remote service's terms. Do not upload modern copyrighted scans or editorial editions without applicable permission. Public-domain authorship does not settle modern-edition rights.

**Remaining before Q0.2 exit**: acquire verified full Quran edition; record independent publisher checksum and license attribution; validate and peer-check exact text; model *real* verse ranges and folio mappings; ingest appropriately licensed tafsir editions with page-anchored citations; add an evidence-based decision ledger and CI engineering/release policy. Human signatures are required only for specific contested editorial or authoritative scholarly actions under [risk-based assurance](../docs/RISK_BASED_ASSURANCE.md), not for technical corpus milestones; existing public content authenticity and rights gates remain in force.

Run `python -m unittest discover -s corpus/tests -v` and `python corpus/registry.py example.json`. The tests use **synthetic fixtures**, not religious text.
